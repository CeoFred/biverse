How it works

  1. Listen

    Connect any sound source: the laptop microphone, a USB mic, or a feed from the sound desk. A live meter shows Biverse can hear.

  2. Recognise

    Offline speech recognition picks out references like “second Timothy three sixteen” or “Psalm one nineteen, verse one hundred and five”. Words such as mark, job and acts are not mistaken for books.

  3. Project

    In Manual mode the verse waits in Preview until you press Go Live. In Auto mode it goes straight to the screen. Biverse follows the reading and moves to the next verse as the preacher finishes each one.

Built for the media desk

Any audio input
Built-in or USB microphones, audio interfaces, mixer line-in, Stereo Mix, VB-Cable or BlackHole.
Accurate with real accents
Kroko is built in for live recognition. Parakeet, a one-click download in Settings, is far better with African and other accents.
Voice navigation
“Next verse”, “stop there”, “continue”, and “let’s read this in the ASV” all work hands-free.
Fast search
Type jn 3:16, 1 cor 13 4-7 or a phrase like for god so loved.
Emphasis
Bold, underline or highlight words of a verse while it is on the projector.
Models in one click
Settings → Models downloads a model with a progress bar, checks it and switches to it. No zip files to find.
Sermon summary
Every service gets a record: the scriptures read, key points and a timeline. You can save it or copy it.
Your translations
KJV, WEB, ASV, YLT and BBE are included. You can import a translation your church is licensed for.
A clean output screen
Borderless on any monitor, with fonts, colours, a shadow, a background image, and Blank and Black.

AI sermon summary

The built-in summary works on every computer. It lists the scriptures, picks the key sentences and builds a timeline. The optional AI pack goes further and writes a short summary in plain prose. It runs on the church computer and nothing is sent anywhere.

The AI pack is not included in the app download. Download it in Settings → Models, or as a zip below. Biverse checks the computer first: if it can’t run the AI summary, it says why instead of downloading or installing it.

Questions

Does Biverse need the internet?

No. Speech recognition, the Bible texts and the AI summary all run on the computer, so a service never needs the internet. Biverse goes online only when you press Download for an optional model, and to check for a new version about twice a day (never while it is listening, and you can turn it off in Settings → Updates). The check reads one small file from GitHub and sends nothing about you. There is no account and no telemetry.

Will it run on our old church laptop?

Most likely. There are builds for Windows 7 SP1 and later, including 32-bit netbooks, and for Macs from macOS 10.15. The window fits a 1024×600 screen. Windows 7 needs update KB2999226.

Can we use the NIV, NKJV or ESV?

These translations are copyrighted, so they can’t be included. If your church holds a licence, import the text with --import-translation. The guide explains how.

What if it shows the wrong verse?

Use Manual mode. Detected verses wait in Preview, and nothing reaches the screen until you press Go Live.

Is it really free?

Yes. Biverse is open source under the MIT licence. The included Bible texts are public domain.
